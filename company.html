Corporate environmental impact

Personal habits matter, but it helps to see them next to the scale of corporate impact. Here's a framework for reading a company's environmental record.

What to look for in a company's environmental disclosures
Category What it measures Where to find it
Emissions (Scope 1, 2, 3) Direct emissions, purchased energy, and full supply-chain emissions Annual sustainability or ESG report
Water use & discharge Water withdrawn from local sources and quality of what's returned Sustainability report, CDP water disclosure
Waste & packaging Total waste generated and share diverted from landfill Sustainability report
Renewable energy share Percent of operations powered by renewable sources Sustainability report, RE100 disclosures
Third-party verification Whether claims are independently audited or self-reported Assurance statement, usually in the report appendix

Reading between the lines

Absolute vs. relative numbers

A falling emissions "intensity" figure can still mean rising total emissions if the company has simply grown faster.

Scope 3 is the big one

For most companies, supply-chain emissions dwarf their own operations — and it's the figure most often left out.

Targets vs. results

A stated goal for 2030 or 2050 says little about current performance. Look for year-over-year progress against it.

This page offers a general framework, not an evaluation of any specific company. For real figures, check a company's own published sustainability report or an independent database such as CDP.
